# Why the Overview takes eleven seconds with 1D selected — 1 September 2026

*Diagnosed against `46d65df`, on a local PostgreSQL 16.13 (Ubuntu 24.04) with the demo household
scaled to the reported shape. Every number below — except the covering-index measurement, whose two index definitions are given
where it is reported — was produced by the scripts in
[`2026-09-01-overview-1d-latency/harness/`](2026-09-01-overview-1d-latency/harness), and that
directory's README is the command sequence that reproduces them from an empty database.*

The fix is approved: [spec 0016](../specs/0016-session-series-running-total.md) is canonical for
what the query becomes and why. This file is the evidence it rests on — what was measured, on what,
and what was tried and rejected. Where the two disagree about the design, the spec wins; where they
disagree about a number, this file is where the number came from.

## What was reported

The Overview with 1D selected takes about eleven seconds to answer, on a household of 21 open
accounts and 97 holdings. Cold load and client-side navigation from Analysis both, so it is not a
document-size or a service-worker effect. Other ranges are unremarkable and Analysis is fine.

## The diagnosis

**One statement, not a pile of them.** `time-overview.ts` runs the queries the Overview loader runs
(`app/routes/overview.tsx:98` and `:135`), in the loader's two waves, and times each. With 1D
selected, one of the six is the whole of the time; every other read on the page answers in tens of
milliseconds, and the same household's 1Y line — 180 dates through `holding_valued_at` — answers in
about a hundred and twenty. The statement is the session series in `readSessionSeries`
(`app/lib/valuation.server.ts:679`), which `chart-series.server.ts` reads for the household surface
and the account surface both.

**The plan is instants × holdings.** The query values every holding at every distinct instant of
the session: for each `as_of` it joins the positions held now and, per pair, runs two correlated
lookups — the latest observation at or before the instant, and the last close strictly before the
session. `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` at the seeded cadence shows the inner join producing 157,140
rows, which is 1,620 × 97 exactly, with the observation lateral executed once per row.

**Why 1,620 and not 27 — the term the cost model missed.** `ARCHITECTURE.md` §10 costed the line at
"~27 instants a session at the seeded cadence", which is the number of *polls* in a session at
fifteen minutes. But `as_of` is the provider's instant for **each instrument** — the moment the
provider says that price was struck, never the poll time (ADR-0006; the header of
`migrations/0009_price_observation.sql` states it as a rule of the schema). A feed stamps each
instrument's last trade separately, so one poll of ninety-eight instruments yields up to ninety-eight
distinct instants, and a session holds of the order of polls × feed instruments. At fifteen minutes
and 98 feed instruments that is 1,620, not 27 — sixty times the assumed count, each multiplied by
the holding count.

The demo household never showed it: `scripts/seed-demo.ts` gives every quoted instrument the same
grid of instants, and its whole log is 222 rows — the count `scale-observations.sql` reported
replacing on a fresh seed. That is why the figure in §10 survived: it was true of the only dataset
anything had been run against.

## What was measured

Local PostgreSQL 16.13 at the image's default settings; the demo seed scaled by
`scale-shape.sql` to 21 open accounts, 97 holdings and 98 feed instruments; the observation log
regenerated by `scale-observations.sql` at each cadence with each instrument carrying its own
seconds on `as_of`. Times are the statement alone, from `psql`'s `\timing`.

| Refresh cadence | Distinct instants in the session | Current query | The rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 minutes (the seeded default) | 1,620 | 3.5 s | 20 ms |
| 5 minutes | 4,740 | 10.1 s | 50 ms |
| 1 minute | 23,460 | 48 s | 200 ms |

The reported eleven seconds is the fifteen-minute row on a slower host, and with more instants than
this harness produces: its seconds are arithmetic (`(instrument_id × 37) % 60`) and collide, which
is why 2,646 observations make 1,620 instants rather than 2,646. A real feed's collide less.

**The log's size is a second-order term.** The same fifteen-minute query over a 42-session log
(111,000 rows) measured 3.6 s, and over a year of sessions (661,500 rows) 3.3–3.6 s across repeated
runs, with one run at 5.3 s. The spread between those runs is larger than the difference the volume
makes: what the query costs is the pairs it forms, not the rows the table holds.

**The rest of the page, for scale.** Every other Overview read measured at or under about 45 ms; 1Y
at about 120 ms; the loader data for the 1,620 chart points, 97,201 bytes.

### The plan, abridged to the lines that carry the cost

```
GroupAggregate  (actual rows=1620)
  ->  Nested Loop Left Join  (actual rows=157140)             -- 1,620 instants x 97 holdings
        ->  Index Scan Backward using price_observation_pkey  (loops=157140)
        ->  Index Scan Backward using price_daily_pkey        (loops=157140)
  Buffers: shared hit≈1,470,000 read=0                        -- JIT on
```

Every probe is already an index scan stopping at its first row. Nothing in the plan is a mistake:
the cost is the number of probes.

## Alternatives measured and rejected

**Covering indexes.** `(instrument_id, as_of) include (price)` and `(instrument_id, date) include
(close)`, turning both laterals into index-only scans, measured **4.5 s against 3.5 s** at fifteen
minutes — slower, and in any case aimed at the cost of one probe rather than at their number.

**Postgres memory and cache settings.** The plan reports 1.47 million shared-buffer *hits* and zero
reads for one render: the working set is already in memory and the time is CPU spent executing the
loop. `shared_buffers`, `work_mem` and `effective_cache_size` have nothing to save here.

**A cache — Redis or an in-process memo.** The line changes on every poll and on every upload or
balance edit, so the cache is invalidated exactly as often as the value it holds changes; at a
one-minute cadence it is cold at almost every visit, and the first render after each poll still pays
the full query. It would also mask an O(instants × holdings) query rather than fix it — the argument
`migrations/0002_holding_valued.sql`'s header and `ARCHITECTURE.md` §10 already make against a
materialised view — and add a second stateful service to a deployment whose whole operating posture
is that the database is the only thing to back up.

## The rewrite, and what makes it the same line

Compute the series as a running total over the session's observations of held instruments, instead
of re-valuing every holding at every instant. The identity: the line at instant *T* is the sum over
holdings of `cast(quantity × price(T) as numeric(20, 4))`, and each holding's `price(T)` is a step
function that moves only when its instrument is observed. So the total at *T* is the total at the
open, plus each observation's new rounded value minus the one it replaced. The differences telescope
exactly in `numeric`, and the rounding stays per holding, which is what makes the sums identical to
the character rather than close. Spec 0016 carries the statement and the reasoning for each of its
parts.

The work becomes each held instrument's observations inside the session's span, read once per
holding through `price_observation_pkey`, plus one sort over the instants and the change instants
together — the right-hand column of the table above.

### Equivalence, and how it was checked

Both statements into temporary tables, then `except` both ways; zero rows either way is the claim.
Four datasets: the scaled household at the seeded cadence; the same with two earlier sessions of
observations, so the opening price comes from an observation rather than a close; that with one held
instrument stripped of every close before the session, so `known` steps mid-session (91 → 94 at
13:30:54, three holdings sharing the instrument); and the same household under a year of sessions.
Zero rows in both directions on all four. `compare.sql` is that check.

### The planner trap the review caught

Written the obvious way, the span's bounds are a `bounds` CTE holding `min(as_of)` and `max(as_of)`,
joined in. Through a join the span reaches the `changes` scan as a join condition, which the planner
does not turn into an index condition, so it sequentially scans the whole observation log —
materialised or not: the diff review measured the CTE inlined, materialised and `not materialized`
at 114–118 ms alike. On one session's log nobody notices; on a year of
sessions (661,500 rows) it is **89 ms and growing with the log from then on** — seconds again inside
a year at a one-minute cadence.

Written instead as `o.as_of >= (select min(as_of) from instants)`, the bound is an init-plan
parameter the planner can put into an index condition: a bitmap index scan on
`price_observation_pkey` per holding, **16 ms** on the same log, and the log's growth stops
mattering. Checked on PostgreSQL 16; the first thing to re-check on the 17 image the deployment runs
is that this plan survives.

## Second run — 2 September 2026, from an empty database

The harness in this directory, run end to end on a fresh `portfolio_bench`: migrate, seed,
`scale-shape.sql`, `scale-observations.sql`, then the measurements. Same host class, PostgreSQL
16.13, session `2026-09-02`, and the worktree still carrying the current query. It reproduced the
shape exactly — 21 open accounts, 97 holdings, 98 feed instruments, 2,646 observation rows, 1,620
distinct instants — and the numbers within run-to-run scatter:

| Measurement | 1 September | 2 September |
|---|---|---|
| Current query, 15 min / 1,620 instants | 3.5 s | 4.0 s |
| Current query, 5 min / 4,740 instants | 10.1 s | 11.4 s |
| Current query, 1 min / 23,460 instants | 48 s | 57.1 s |
| Rewrite, 15 / 5 / 1 min | 20 / 50 / 200 ms | 21 / 48 / 196 ms |
| Overview loader, 1D, `chartSeries` | — | 3,724 ms of a 3,753 ms wall |
| Overview loader, other reads | ≤ ~45 ms | ≤ 52 ms |
| Overview loader, 1Y | ~120 ms | 185 ms |
| Loader-data bytes, 1,620 points | 97,201 | 97,201 |
| Inner rows in the current plan | 157,140 | 157,140 |
| Buffers for one render, current plan | ~1.47 M hits, 0 reads | 1,124,108 hits, 0 reads |
| `except` both ways | 0 / 0 on four datasets | 0 / 0 on three |
| `bounds` CTE against scalar subqueries | 89 ms / 16 ms at 661,500 rows | 91.7 ms / 17.9 ms at 470,988 rows |

Three differences worth naming rather than smoothing over. The second run's log at `-v days=250` is
250 calendar days, which is 178 trading sessions and 470,988 rows, not the first run's 661,500;
`-v days=350`, fifty weeks, reproduces those. Its
current-query plan put the daily-close lateral behind a `Memoize` node (157,140 loops, 50 misses),
so only the observation lateral probed the index 157,140 times, and the buffer count is
correspondingly lower — the shape of the cost is unchanged. And JIT accounted for 294 ms of the
4,141 ms the plan reported.

The three datasets of the second run's equivalence check were the seeded cadence on one session, the
seeded cadence under 178 sessions, and the one-minute cadence (23,460 rows compared): zero rows in
both directions on each.

The plan check spec 0016 asks for passes on the second run: at 470,988 observation rows the
`changes` join is a **Bitmap Index Scan on `price_observation_pkey`** (`loops=97`), execution
17.9 ms. On a single session's log the same query sequentially scans it instead: the whole table is
24 buffers, so a scan is the cheaper plan whatever the predicate says — which is why the plan check
needs the year of sessions behind it.

## What is approved, and what is still a decision

**Approved:** [spec 0016](../specs/0016-session-series-running-total.md) — the rewrite, its tests,
and the `ARCHITECTURE.md` passages whose cost model this diagnosis falsified. No migration, no new
index, no change to any route or to `chartSeries`.

**Still a decision, and deliberately left open:** how many points a 1D line should have. Today it is
one per observed instant — 1,620 points and 97,201 bytes at the seeded cadence, 23,460 points and
about 1.4 MB at one minute on the same per-point size. The rewrite makes those points cheap to
*compute* and leaves the count exactly where it was; bucketing a session's instants to the poll
would divide both figures by the instrument count, and would change what 1D means (ADR-0006). Once
the query is tens of milliseconds, the payload is the largest remaining term on the page, and
response compression — the Caddyfile has no `encode` directive — becomes worth its own look.

## Reproducing this

```sh
psql postgres://portfolio:portfolio@127.0.0.1:55432/portfolio_test -c 'create database portfolio_bench'
printf 'DATABASE_URL=postgres://portfolio:portfolio@127.0.0.1:55432/portfolio_bench\n' > .env.bench
node --env-file=.env.bench ./server/migrate.ts
node --env-file=.env.bench ./scripts/seed-demo.ts

DB=postgres://portfolio:portfolio@127.0.0.1:55432/portfolio_bench
H=docs/research/2026-09-01-overview-1d-latency/harness

psql "$DB" -f $H/scale-shape.sql
psql "$DB" -v cadence=15 -v days=1 -f $H/scale-observations.sql
SESSION=$(psql "$DB" -tAc 'select max(market_date) from price_observation')

DATABASE_URL=$DB node $H/time-overview.ts 1d 1y
psql "$DB" -v session=$SESSION -f $H/compare.sql
psql "$DB" -v session=$SESSION -v prefix='explain (analyze, buffers)' -f $H/session-current.sql
```

The full sequence, including the dials for the other cadences and the year of sessions the plan
check needs, is in
[`harness/README.md`](2026-09-01-overview-1d-latency/harness/README.md). Both writing scripts refuse
any database whose name does not end in `_test`, `_demo` or `_bench`.
